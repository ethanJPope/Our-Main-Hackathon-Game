# Legacy Visual-Test Prompt Set — V1–V9 Archive

**Status:** Superseded by [CleanRebootPrompt.md](CleanRebootPrompt.md). Do not use this file for new visual work. It remains intact as decision history for the discarded V1–V9 direction.

These prompts were the reproducible brief for the original four art-compass tests. Their scene lore remains useful, but their rendering, material, tree, grass, outline, and surface rules are no longer active.

## Shared visual lock

Use case: stylized-concept  
Asset type: mobile game environment visual test  
Input image: Image 1 is the rendering, foliage-density, color, and lighting reference; do not copy its exact layout or objects.  
Style/medium: polished in-engine-looking 3D game screenshot; low-poly geometry built from thin vegetation cards and simple irregular faceted solids; one uniform base color per separate geometric piece; grounded stylization; saturated natural palette; layered vegetation; no painted surface texture; soft atmospheric depth.  
Camera: 16:9 landscape; elevated soft-perspective isometric gameplay camera; approximately 35-degree elevation and 40-degree field of view; player slightly below screen center; readable mobile-game scale.  
Lighting: bright midday sun, cool ambient fill, soft readable shadows, irregular sun patches, subtle distance haze, only subtle depth of field.  
Character lock: same compact traveler in every frame; simple faceted expressive face; short one-shoulder mantle, patched tunic, practical boots, offset belt gear, repaired slightly oversized one-handed sword; thin dark local-color outline only around living characters.  
World lock: original designs; organic functional asymmetry; rustic timber, fieldstone, rope, cloth, and iron; rare chipped crystal-blade beacon forms; fractures and imperfect handwritten script as recurring magical motifs.  
Construction lock: complexity by aggregation, never by individual asset complexity. Vegetation is visually dense but physically thin. Conifers use nearly two-dimensional rectangular, trapezoidal, or lightly jagged foliage cards stacked into soft triangular silhouettes; the cards have almost no edge thickness. Every grass blade is one continuous flat low-poly ribbon/card with a clearly visible broad face and paper-thin depth. Lock its width and short-to-medium height to V8. Its two long edges rise straight and vertically from the base with no lean, splay, bend, arc, or curl. Preserve varied heights, slight width variation, overlapping rooted clumps, and visible ground gaps so straightness does not become a uniform wall. Keep ends flat or lightly angled without showing a horizontal top surface. Grass must never become uniformly tall pickets, bamboo-like posts, cylinders, tubes, square-prism cross sections, visible top caps, joined segments, crossed-card stars, edge-on hairlines, circles, dots, or fuzzy texture. Groundcover may use overlapping flat leaf cards, dots, circles, squares, and short rectangles only in visually separate low patches. Flowers use tiny crosses, circles, diamonds, or four-square clusters. Rocks are simple irregular five-to-eight-face low-poly forms, with a mix of exposed and partly buried stones. Fallen logs have a roughly squared but gently tapered or bent low-sided profile, never a perfect prism. Buildings use shallow broad wall planes, a few timber lengths, and one-to-three roof planes with slightly skewed corners. Damage removes or tilts whole pieces instead of adding fine rubble. The guardian uses a small set of large irregular boulder masses.  
Material lock: every separate primitive or geometric piece has exactly one uniform intrinsic base/albedo color. Adjacent pieces may use different colors from a small palette. Real lighting, face normals, cast shadows, and ambient occlusion may shade that color, but do not paint any gradient, mottling, noise, grain, stain, edge wear, or texture across the piece. Moss, repairs, cloth patches, and color accents must be separate solid-color geometry rather than marks painted onto another shape.  
Color lock: living greens and warm earth; cyan-white beacon energy; localized bruised violet-black corruption; violet-blue-white flower accents.  
Constraints: no HUD, no text, no watermark, no screen border; no environment outlines; no voxel grid; no thick extruded foliage tiers; no square-rod or edge-on hairline grass; no perfect-cube rock field; no miniature diorama platform; no photorealism; no plastic toy look; no exaggerated cartoon proportions; no heavy bloom; no global gloom; no magical route markers; no color texture maps, albedo gradients, procedural mottling, grain, stains, painted edge accents, or decorative per-face color; no modeled needles, smooth sphere bushes, bark grooves, root webs, individual petals, tiny cracks, individual shingles, stone-by-stone masonry, wheel spokes, filigree, debris carpets, or recognizable characters, symbols, structures, enemies, items, and interface elements from existing games.

## Test 01 — Village Spawn

Primary request: Show the player's first view in a destroyed farming village several years after a human raid.  
Scene/backdrop: open farms built from broad ground-color strips and broken crop rows of thin plant cards. Each ruined house uses only a few shallow, slightly skewed wall planes, simple timber lengths, and two or three broad thin roof planes; old raid damage is shown by missing, tilted, or blackened whole pieces, plus simple broken fence lengths and one broad cool-ash patch. No individual shingles, masonry, cart wheels, tiny debris, perfect toy blocks, or active fire. A dense evergreen forest of almost paper-thin foliage-card trees begins in the middle distance. A damaged regional crystal tower made from a few large chipped prisms stands on a simple irregular low-poly hill shrine. Far beyond it, one monumental fractured crystal blade pierces a mountain summit and remains clearly visible against the sky.  
Subject: the traveler stands slightly below center. Near the village, an intimidating dormant or slowly stirring hunched guardian is visible, around four times the traveler's height, constructed from roughly twelve-to-twenty large asymmetrical irregular faceted boulders with long arms, uneven shoulders, a few flat moss patches, and restrained violet-black fractures. It must feel too dangerous to fight immediately without dominating the entire frame.  
Composition: clear traversable ground and sunlight subtly lead away from the guardian toward the forest bypass. Keep foreground and player crisp; retain open space for camera rotation.  
Mood: wonder and unease in bright daylight; human destruction is sad but old, while the wider world remains beautiful.

## Test 02 — Lush Forest Traversal

Primary request: Show normal exploration through the first region's healthy evergreen forest.  
Scene/backdrop: dense layered conifers whose foliage is visibly assembled from stacked, offset, nearly two-dimensional rectangular, trapezoidal, or lightly jagged cards; card edges are hair-thin and each separate card uses one solid green chosen from a two-to-four-green tree palette. Fill the ground with loose overlapping clumps of flat low-poly ribbon grass. Lock every blade's broad visible-face width and short-to-medium height to V8, then make its long edges straight and world-vertical with no lean or bend. Preserve varied blade heights and slight width variation, and keep most blades ankle-to-low-calf height near the player with visible ground between clumps. Keep overlapping flat leaf/dot groundcover visually separate from the grass layer. Add simple irregular five-to-eight-face stones, separate solid-color moss pieces, and one low-sided fallen log with a roughly squared but gently tapered or bent profile, an angled end, and separate thin moss pieces. Place some rocks fully above ground and bury others one-third to two-thirds into the terrain like the supplied forest reference. Use sparse violet, blue, white, and yellow flower crosses/dots. Every geometric piece has one uniform base color; lighting supplies all within-piece light/dark variation. Do not include rescaled grass, uniformly tall picket walls, bamboo-like grass, visible top caps, segmented stems, circular grass, edge-on hairline grass, thick rods, crossed-card stars, a modeled stump, thick tree tiers, perfect-cube stones, smooth sphere shrubs, bark detail, roots, needles, complex rocks, or painted textures. Use rich grouping of neighboring solid colors and bright irregular sunlight across the clearing.  
Subject: the same traveler moves through a readable natural route. Include one or two small familiar stylized animals at a distance, such as a fox and birds, without making them focal characters.  
Composition: foliage creates a dense frame around a clearer traversable center; compressed grass, warmer ground, leaning plants, and sunlight indicate the route without signs or glow. Preserve enough negative space around the player for mobile readability.  
Mood: inviting, alive, quiet, and full of discovery. This is the clearest test of the supplied reference's rendering qualities while remaining an original scene.

V3 preservation note: retain the first-pass traveler's design, gameplay camera, central route, bright sun-patch rhythm, and overall green palette. Correct V2's blockiness by changing vegetation thickness and controlled prop irregularity—not by adding geometry or texture detail.

V4 calibration note: V4 proved that the grass must be rectangular but made the visible faces too narrow, so it remains a rejected hairline-width reference.

V5 grass-isolation note: change only grass orientation and visible width. Preserve every tree, bush, flower, rock, log, character, camera, light, color, and object position. Each blade must present its broad face to the camera while remaining almost depthless.

V6 material-isolation note: use the V5 geometry as the intended immutable base and change only material treatment to one uniform base color per piece. Because the generated V6 frame drifted some geometry, use it only as a material-treatment reference, not as the combined geometry master.

V7 grass-shape note: V7 replaced V6's rigid bamboo-like posts with varied flat ribbon blades using the user-supplied grass asset as a shape reference. It remains useful as the narrower-width comparison.

V8 grass-width note: change only V7 blade face width, increasing it about 30–40 percent. Keep V7 heights, base locations, lean, bends, clump footprints, density, solid-color assignments, camera, lighting, and every non-grass object. Ethan approved V8's dimensions.

V9 grass-orientation note: use V8 as the immutable dimension source. Remove only blade lean and curvature so both long edges rise straight and vertically from each existing base. Preserve V8 width, height category, paper-thin depth, ends, count, base positions, spacing, density, clump footprints, colors, camera, lighting, and all non-grass content. V9 is the current orientation candidate.

## Test 03 — Quarry Guardian Reveal

Primary request: Show the moment the traveler crests a grassy hill and discovers the guardian's true boss space hidden below.  
Scene/backdrop: a believable abandoned quarry bowl concealed behind the hill; irregular terraced stone cuts, broken extraction faces, overgrown ramps, exposed roots, moss, scattered old work remnants, and pooled dust. Midday sky remains bright above, while high quarry walls and overhangs create cooler environmental shadow inside.  
Subject: the same traveler appears small at the near rim. The hunched living-mountain guardian stands below, approximately four times taller, asymmetrical and readable, with long stone arms, low head, uneven shoulders, moss and packed soil. A separate stolen cyan-white crystal shard rests clearly on a fractured stone support behind or beside the guardian; it is not embedded in the boss. Localized violet-black corruption marks the guardian, the shard's immediate surroundings, and a few quarry fractures.  
Composition: use a modest boss-view zoom-out while preserving the same gameplay camera family. The hill edge creates the reveal; the quarry must not look like a perfect circular arena. Keep the guardian, traveler, and separate shard readable as three distinct value silhouettes.  
Mood: environmental darkness and intimidating scale without changing the global weather or turning the whole image gloomy.

## Test 04 — Beacon Restoration Comparison

Primary request: Create one 16:9 before-and-after comparison of the same village-and-fields composition, split into two equal vertical halves with no labels or text. A thin neutral center seam is acceptable.  
Scene/backdrop: reuse the ruined timber-and-fieldstone village, farm fields, hill shrine, regional crystal tower, evergreen forest line, and distant mountain crystal blade from Test 01. Camera, geometry, player position, ruins, and framing must match across both halves.  
Left half, before: damaged dark regional beacon; interrupted gray-green growth near corruption wounds; dead crop rows; fewer flowers and no visible wildlife; localized violet-black fractures and faint soot-like haze; slightly flatter local color.  
Right half, after: regional beacon emits stable cyan-white light; violet corruption has retracted into inert scars; crops and grasses are fuller; flowers and familiar birds return; forest and field color are richer; local bounce light is clearer. Burned houses, collapsed roofs, broken fences, and all human-caused damage remain unchanged.  
Subject: the same traveler is present at the same scale and position in both halves.  
Composition: make the transformation unmistakable through foliage density, wildlife, value, and state changes—not color alone. Keep the split clean but natural, with no infographic graphics or captions.  
Mood: restoration is hopeful and powerful while respecting the permanence of human history.
